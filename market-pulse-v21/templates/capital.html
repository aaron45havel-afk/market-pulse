How to read this

Stock returns are each screen's own estimate — Compounders' growth, buybacks, dividend and multiple drift; the Aristocrats' yield plus dividend growth; Lynch's earnings yield plus growth; Quiet Value's earnings yield; Schloss's discount to tangible book closing halfway — blended toward the market return by the weight you set, because one company's estimate is noisier than the market's. They are taxed as if held in a taxable account (dividends yearly, gains at the end of the hold); the Roth step puts the top pick where neither is taxed.

BRRRR and flips are conditional. There are no fixer sale prices in the data, so the board shows the after-tax target you set, reached only if you buy at or below the price /headroom solves — and the share of the median that price needs is how hard the deal is to find. House hacks are owner-occupied, so only ZIPs within your radius of home count; their return is on the cash to close and includes the rent you stop paying (once — not if you already own where you live), the loan paid down in year one, and your appreciation setting. Real estate's share of your net worth is capped by the glide path you set; a property that would take it over the cap at the net worth you will have when it is funded reads "over cap" and takes no money.

Friction is subtracted from the after-tax return: buying and selling spread over the hold, your hours at your rate as a share of the money in the asset, and — for anything that needs more cash than you have free beyond the emergency fund — the average month the money sits in T-bills while it is saved, at the T-bill rate after tax. Mortgage rate {{ board.mortgage_rate }}% (Freddie Mac, weekly). Every figure here is an estimate, not a promise.
