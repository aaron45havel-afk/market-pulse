The multiple is a choice. The growth is a bet.

UBS HOLT cut 34 years of US large caps by starting multiple against the sales growth they went on to deliver. The column is forward growth — growth that has not happened — so no screen can sort on it without sorting on hindsight. This page treats it as a probability instead, measured from how trailing growth actually rolled forward in our own universe.

“It's hard to overcome a high starting multiple, even when future growth is robust. The average 50x multiple stock underperforms, even when you only sample companies that also delivered at least 20% p.a. growth.” UBS HOLT · largest 1000 US companies · 1990–2024

What this cannot tell you

The multiple is a proxy. HOLT's rows are Economic P/E — CFROI-based, inflation-adjusted, gross-asset, and proprietary. We stand {{ proxy }} in its place. Both are cash-based so the ordering carries, but the boundaries do not transfer literally: a 50x Economic P/E is not a 50x {{ proxy }}.

Both datasets are survivorship-biased, the same way. HOLT's footnote admits its table includes only companies that survived the five subsequent years. Our transition matrix has the same hole — it is measured on companies alive today with fifteen years of filings, so the 20%+ grower that collapsed and delisted is not in it. Both sets of numbers are upper bounds. Neither is corrected here, because correcting them would mean inventing a number.

And this is a base rate, not a forecast. It says what happened on average to stocks that started where this one starts. It knows nothing about this particular company.
