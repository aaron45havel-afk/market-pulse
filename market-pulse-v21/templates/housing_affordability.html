How this is measured

  • Payment: principal and interest on a 30-year fixed loan at {{ down_pct|int }}% down (no PMI), the ZIP's property tax rate, and the state's average homeowner (HO-3) premium scaled to the price — the ZIP page's owner card.
  • Today: Zillow's typical home value (ZHVI) for {{ meta.get("zhvi_last_month", "") }}; Freddie Mac's 30-year rate {{ pct(n.rate, 2) }} ({{ rate_date }}); Census {{ meta.get("acs_vintage", "") }} median household income (in {{ c.acs_end_year }} dollars) brought to {{ c.cpi_latest_month }} dollars by CPI ×{{ "%.3f"|format(c.income_to_today) }} — this assumes no real income growth since {{ c.acs_end_year }}.
  • 2019, fixed: Zillow's 2019 average value, Freddie Mac's 2019 average rate {{ pct(c.rate19, 2) }}, and Census {{ meta.get("acs19_vintage", "") }} income in 2019 dollars. The tax rate is today's; insurance is today's premium deflated to 2019 by CPI (premiums rose faster than prices, so 2019's payment is if anything overstated).
  • Affordable price: the price whose payment takes exactly {{ share|int }}% of the median income at today's rate.
  • States and the US are a typical household: the household-weighted median price, income and tax rate across ZIPs that have every figure in both years — the same ZIPs, weighted the same way, at both ends.
  • The ZIP map colours every ZIP by its own figures, and its "median" counts each ZIP once — rural ZIPs included — so it reads lower than this page's typical household, which weights ZIPs by households.
  • Why it changed: Shapley decomposition — each factor's average effect over every order in which the four could change, so the parts sum to the total exactly.
  • This replaces the "fair value" page, whose verdict depended on a baseline that rolled forward each month and compared a statewide index today with a median-ZIP figure then.
Sources: Zillow Research (ZHVI); Freddie Mac PMMS and BLS CPI-U via FRED ({{ (macro or {}).get("source", "") }}); U.S. Census Bureau ACS 5-year estimates; state insurance averages (re_assumptions.py). Not financial advice.
