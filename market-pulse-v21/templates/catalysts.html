What's watched, and what each one means

Completion rates are priors, not measurements — they encode structure (is the cash already there? is there a financing condition? does the buyer control the vote?) and are meant to be overridden per deal.

Why the ranking is annualized and not the headline spread. A 3% spread closing in 30 days is ~43%/yr. A 20% spread resolving over three years is ~6%/yr. The second looks better and isn't close.

Why the round trip comes off first. A 6% spread in a name with a 4% bid-ask is a 2% trade. These situations live in illiquid securities, which is exactly where that cost is worst — see the quiet-value board for the dark-days and daily-volume figures on the same names.

Why tax isn't a footnote. A 60-day event is ordinary income. At 37%, a 25% gross return nets 15.75% — barely at a 15% after-tax bar — while the same 25% held thirteen months nets 20%. The code is paying you to be slow.

Why dollars sit beside the percentage. An odd-lot tender is capped at 99 shares. At a $2 spread that's $198, however well it annualizes. Ranking on percentage alone fills the queue with plays that are mathematically beautiful and economically irrelevant.
