How the math works — and the two questions everyone asks

The solve. For each market: fixer entry = median × {{ '%.2f'|format(calib.x) }}, renovated ARV = median × {{ '%.2f'|format(calib.y) }} (verified July-2026 calibration: Zillow hedonic fixer discounts, distressed-sale research, at/below ATTOM's observed 25.4% national flip ROI). Rehab from the validated 107-market cost model. {% if mode == 'brrrr' %}BRRRR: hard-money acquisition ({{ '%.2f'|format(fin.hm_rate * 100) }}%, LTARV-capped), DSCR cash-out refi at month 6 ({{ '%.2f'|format(fin.refi_rate * 100) }}%, 75% ARV, rent must cover the payment), {{ hold_years }}-yr hold at ZORI median rent, then sale — after-tax IRR on a monthly grid: 27.5-yr depreciation, suspended passive losses, non-taxable refi cash, recapture + LTCG + NIIT + state at exit.{% else %}Flip: dealer taxation — ordinary income + self-employment tax, no capital-gains rates.{% endif %} The engine bisects to the highest purchase price where the after-tax return ≥ {{ '%.0f'|format(target) }}% and {% if mode == 'brrrr' %}the deal's total after-tax profit over the {{ hold_years }}-yr hold{% else %}after-tax profit{% endif %} ≥ ${{ '{:,.0f}'.format(profit_floor) }} — in yield pockets that $25k comes from years of cash flow, not a day-one equity pop, and the gate honors that.

"Is 14% even the right bar?" Anchor it to your debt: money costs you {{ '%.2f'|format(fin.hm_rate * 100) }}% (hard money) to {{ '%.2f'|format(fin.refi_rate * 100) }}% (DSCR) here. Levered equity below the debt rate means the leverage is working against you — so 5–10% would be irrational at today's rates: you'd be taking construction risk to earn less than your lender. The rational floor is debt rate + a real spread for the work and risk; {{ '%.0f'|format(target) }}% after-tax ≈ 18–19% pre-tax ≈ debt + ~10%. If rates fall, lower the bar with them — edit the rate and target above and the whole board re-solves.

Competition. Two places: the Competition column is each market's price-trajectory read (accelerating = you're bidding against people; declining = negotiable but values falling — take the ▼ veto seriously). The Competition ±x knob shifts what fixers actually cost: in a no-bidders market fixers sell further below median (helps you); in bidding wars the discount evaporates. Bounds follow the calibration's months-of-supply rule; a live inventory feed is the roadmap upgrade.

First-pass screen on market medians — never an appraisal, never tax advice. A green market can hold bad deals and a red market great ones; the ZIP drill-down and the address underwriter are the next two clicks.
