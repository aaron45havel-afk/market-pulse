How the math works — and the two questions everyone asks

The solve. For each market: renovated ARV = median × {{ '%.2f'|format(calib.y) }} (the July-2026 calibration's {{ scope }} premium); rehab from the 107-market cost model. {% if mode == 'brrrr' %}BRRRR: hard-money purchase ({{ '%.2f'|format(fin.hm_rate * 100) }}%, LTARV-capped); remodel, then rented from the next month at the market rent less {{ '%.0f'|format(vacancy * 100) }}% vacancy while a DSCR cash-out refi seasons ({{ '%.2f'|format(fin.refi_rate * 100) }}%, the smaller of 75% of ARV and what the rent carries); {{ hold_years }}-year hold at Zillow median rent; sale at ARV grown {{ ('%+.1f'|format(appr))|replace('-', '−') }}%/yr. After-tax IRR on a monthly grid: depreciation from the month it is rented, rental losses carried forward against later rental income and released at the sale, refi points amortized and the rest deducted at payoff, non-taxable refi cash, recapture + LTCG + NIIT + state at exit.{% else %}Flip: dealer taxation — ordinary income + self-employment tax, no capital-gains rates.{% endif %} The engine bisects to the highest purchase price where the after-tax return ≥ {{ '%.0f'|format(target) }}% and {% if mode == 'brrrr' %}the deal's total after-tax profit over the {{ hold_years }}-yr hold{% else %}after-tax profit{% endif %} ≥ ${{ '{:,.0f}'.format(profit_floor) }}; Set by names the one a dollar more would break.

"Is 14% even the right bar?" Anchor it to your debt: money costs you {{ '%.2f'|format(fin.hm_rate * 100) }}% (hard money) to {{ '%.2f'|format(fin.refi_rate * 100) }}% (DSCR) here. Levered equity below the debt rate means the leverage is working against you — so 5–10% would be irrational at today's rates: you'd be taking construction risk to earn less than your lender. The rational floor is debt rate + a real spread for the work and risk; {{ '%.0f'|format(target) }}% after-tax ≈ 18–19% pre-tax ≈ debt + ~10%. If rates fall, lower the bar with them — edit the rate and target above and the whole board re-solves.

Competition. Read it from the listings, not the price trend: long and lengthening days on market and a rising share of price cuts are a buyer's market where fixers negotiate; short and shortening ones mean you're bidding against people.

First-pass screen on market medians — never an appraisal, never tax advice. Any market can hold good and bad deals; the ZIP drill-down and the address underwriter are the next two clicks.
