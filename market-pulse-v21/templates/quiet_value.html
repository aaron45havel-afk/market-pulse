Read this before acting on it. The research return for this cohort is gross of trading costs, on an equal-weighted basket of a few hundred names rebalanced annually. Round-trip spread on a low-turnover micro-cap is routinely 2–5%, and it is worst in exactly the cell being selected for — so a meaningful slice of the premium is payment for a cost you would actually pay. Illiquidity premia are also skewed: a minority of names carry the result, which a concentrated portfolio does not replicate.

Turnover is on shares outstanding, not float, to match the research. Micro-caps often carry 40–70% insider ownership, so a closely-held company whose float trades briskly will still read as quiet here. The dark days and days to build columns are the ones that answer whether you can trade it.

An unreported metric is never a pass. Sparse disclosure is not random in this universe — it correlates with the companies you would least like a screen to wave through. Unknowns show as a dashed box and are excluded from the denominator, which is why the column reads passed / known rather than a percentage. {% if meta %}
